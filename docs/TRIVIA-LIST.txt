1. Though all his accomplishments make Ezra Meeker seem larger than life, he was actually how tall?

   a) About five feet one inch  [CORRECT]
   b) Average height for 1850s emigrants
   c) Tall enough to see over oxen without standing on a crate
   d) Well over six feet, like a mountain man

   Teach: Ezra was notably short — a 5′1″ fireplug who was still taller than his bride, Eliza Jane, who stood just 4′8″.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. The Meeker family's original overland emigration to Oregon Territory is associated with which era?

   a) 1810s fur trade only
   b) 1898 gold rush only
   c) 1852 overland migration  [CORRECT]
   d) 1920s auto tourism only

   Teach: The 1852 overland migration is the chapter that defines the Meekers — the great 'flood' year of Oregon Trail travel.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. At journey's end, many families faced land policy questions. A common tension was:

   a) Land was assigned by dice at The Dalles
   b) Free land with no paperwork anywhere
   c) Only British crown grants mattered
   d) Donation land claims vs later fees and filings as territories matured  [CORRECT]

   Teach: Oregon's donation land era and later Washington filings are both part of how Ezra's generation actually secured farms.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. How old would a pioneer need to be to file for a donation land claim?

   a) 21 years old  [CORRECT]
   b) 18 years old
   c) 34 years old
   d) 42 years old

   Teach: Claimants had to be at least 21 — the age of majority in the 1850s — before they could file.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. What major river did pioneers need to cross to proceed along the Oregon Trail?

   a) The Columbia River
   b) The Mississippi River
   c) The Sweetwater River  [CORRECT]
   d) The Colorado River

   Teach: The Sweetwater, with its granite and diorite reaches, was a defining crossing — and the river where Ezra's youngest brother drowned.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

6. How might a pioneer have crossed a river along the Oregon Trail?

   a) Taken a ferry  [CORRECT]
   b) Swam across
   c) Gone around it
   d) Waited for dry season

   Teach: Typically pioneers could ford the river and risk losing life, livestock, and cargo; pay a large fee for a ferry; or caulk their wagons and float them across.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

7. What is the role of a wagon master?

   a) Fix the wagons
   b) Wrangle the wagons
   c) Guide pioneers over the correct trails  [CORRECT]
   d) A professional gambler and card shark

   Teach: A wagon master knew the route — ground speed, river crossings, camps — and guided the company safely over the correct trails.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

8. What year did Washington become a state?

   a) 1853
   b) 1889  [CORRECT]
   c) 1890
   d) 1989

   Teach: Washington joined the Union on November 11, 1889 — four decades after the Meekers crossed the plains.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

9. When did Washington become a territory?

   a) 1492
   b) 1853  [CORRECT]
   c) 1862
   d) 1890

   Teach: Congress carved Washington out of the northern half of Oregon Territory in 1853.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

10. True or False: Washington was once a part of the Oregon Territory.

   a) True  [CORRECT]
   b) False

   Teach: What is now Washington State was once a part of the Oregon Territory.

11. Where did Ezra and Eliza Jane Meeker land after their journey on the Oregon Trail?

   a) El Paso, Texas
   b) Portland, Oregon  [CORRECT]
   c) Puyallup, Washington
   d) Sacramento, California

   Teach: Eliza Jane was so sick on arrival that she had to be carried from the ferry to their lodgings. The Meekers didn't stay long — there was little work and few places to live.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

12. How many family members did Ezra Meeker lose on the Oregon Trail?

   a) 2  [CORRECT]
   b) 7
   c) 1
   d) 4

   Teach: Ezra lost his mother Phoebe, who died of cholera, and his youngest brother Usual, who drowned in the Sweetwater River.

13. What year did Phoebe Meeker, Ezra Meeker's mother, die on the Oregon Trail?

   a) 1852
   b) 1900
   c) 1854  [CORRECT]
   d) 1860

   Teach: Phoebe Meeker died just outside Fort Laramie after contracting cholera.

   a) Corning, New York

15. On the 1852 trail, Ezra and Eliza Jane's baby Marion was:

   a) Not born until Oregon
   b) Seven weeks old when they left Iowa  [CORRECT]
   c) A teenager driving oxen
   d) Left behind with relatives

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

16. When the Meekers reached Portland in October 1852, they had:

   a) $3.25 in their pockets  [CORRECT]
   b) 23 chickens
   c) A cabin already built
   d) A land patent already

   Teach: Nearly broke after the trail — Ezra worked the Mary Melville wharf for $40 while his brother Oliver ran a boarding house.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

17. Ezra Meeker began his famous Oregon Trail Monument Expedition at about age:

   a) Never retraced it
   b) 25 — before hops
   c) 90 only by rail
   d) 76 — by ox cart first  [CORRECT]

   Teach: At 76 he launched the Monument Expedition (1906–1908) — and went on to travel the trail by wagon, automobile, and airplane.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

18. True or False: Horses were the most cost-effective animal to pull covered wagons.

   a) True
   b) False  [CORRECT]

   Teach: Oxen were the workhorses — hardier, able to pull more weight, less likely to be stolen, and able to pull loads for longer stretches.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

19. In what year did the remaining members of Ezra Meeker's family travel over the Oregon Trail?

   a) 1854  [CORRECT]
   b) 1862
   c) 1900
   d) 1842

   Teach: The rest of the family crossed in 1854, two years after Ezra made the trip.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

20. Ezra Meeker first saw what is now present-day Puyallup in:

   a) 1890
   b) 1970
   c) April 1853  [CORRECT]
   d) 1926 only

   Teach: Scouting Puget Sound in 1853, brothers Ezra and Oliver entered the Puyallup River — the huge trees discouraged them then.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

21. In order to bring the rest of his family west, Ezra Meeker:

   a) Bought them a train ticket
   b) Wrote and told them not to come
   c) Sent his brother Oliver back over the trail to guide them  [CORRECT]

22. Ezra donated his 1906 covered wagon to the Washington State Historical Society in:

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

24. In what year did Ezra Meeker first travel the Oregon Trail?

   d) 1852  [CORRECT]

   a) To find gold
   b) To establish new settlements
   c) To preserve its history and mark the trail  [CORRECT]

28. True or False: The family of Martin Ballard traveled on the Oregon Trail with the Meekers in 1852.

   a) True  [CORRECT]
   b) False

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

29. How many people are thought to have died on the Oregon Trail?

   a) Under 50 people
   b) Around 20,000–30,000 people  [CORRECT]
   c) Around 100 people
   d) Around 1,000–3,000 people

   Teach: Historians estimate 20,000 to 30,000 people likely died along the trail. Many graves went unmarked, had markers moved, or were marked with biodegradable wood — making exact numbers hard to pin down.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

30. What was a realistic daily water requirement for one ox on a hot trail day?

   a) Roughly 8–10 gallons  [CORRECT]
   b) Under 1 gallon
   c) 2–3 gallons
   d) 15–20 gallons

   Teach: A working ox drinks plenty — roughly 8–10 gallons on a hot day, and watering stops drove the whole daily travel schedule.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

31. Which disease terrorized wagon trains in many trail-era diaries?

   a) The Plague
   b) Dysentery  [CORRECT]
   c) Covid
   d) Fevers

   Teach: Dysentery — and fellow water-borne ills like cholera — filled trail diaries and graves.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

32. Which Wyoming landmark did emigrants strive to reach 'by the Fourth'?

   a) Independence Rock  [CORRECT]
   b) Scott's Bluff
   c) Chimney Rock
   d) Devil's Gate

   Teach: Independence Rock was the trail's great milestone — parties pushed hard to reach it by the Fourth of July.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

33. True or False: Pioneers typically rode all the way to Oregon in their wagons.

   a) True
   b) False  [CORRECT]

   Teach: Wagons were packed with most families' possessions and had no springs, so pioneers of all ages typically walked alongside or behind them.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

34. True or False: In hot, dry climates, pioneer women would take off their heavy dresses and wear only their underclothes on the trail.

   a) True
   b) False  [CORRECT]

   Teach: A few small groups of ladies experimented with bloomer-style puffy pants, but most pioneer women wore lighter cotton or calico travel dresses with shorter hems.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

35. True or False: Pioneers burned buffalo chips along the trail to keep warm and to cook meals.

   a) True  [CORRECT]
   b) False

   Teach: With resources scarce and meals to cook, pioneers made do with what was on hand — dried buffalo dung burned well.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

   a) The Donner Party
   b) The Longmire-Biles Party  [CORRECT]

37. Covered wagons typically travel how fast?

   a) 2 miles per hour  [CORRECT]
   b) 20 miles per hour
   c) 50 miles per hour
   d) 5 miles per hour

   Teach: A loaded wagon crept along at about 2 miles per hour — a walking pace, mile after mile.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

38. What stunt made headlines on Meeker's 1920s Oregon Trail expedition?

   a) Only traveling by submarine
   b) Walking every mile barefoot
   c) Flying part of the route by airplane in his nineties  [CORRECT]
   d) Refusing to speak in public

   Teach: One of Meeker's favorite ways to travel — the 1924 trip took 23 hours at around 100 mph with five stops, a journey his wagon had made in five months at 2 mph.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

39. True or False: Axles on wagons broke more often than Native raids on wagon parties.

   a) True  [CORRECT]
   b) False

   Teach: Despite the movies, attacks on wagon parties by Native Americans were the exception rather than the rule — broken axles were far more common.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

40. True or False: Oregon Trail guidebooks were the best way to navigate the trail.

   a) True
   b) False  [CORRECT]

   Teach: Guidebooks were often wildly inaccurate — outdated information, occasionally dangerous advice, and some written with no knowledge of the actual trail, often as pure marketing devices.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

41. Who was the most famous Oregon Trail pioneer?

   a) Daniel Boone
   b) Mark Twain
   c) Taylor Swift
   d) Ezra Meeker  [CORRECT]

   Teach: For the monument crusade, the retracings, and the newsreel fame, no pioneer is more tied to the trail's memory than Ezra Meeker.

42. In his old age, Meeker lobbied hardest for governments to:

   a) Mark the Oregon Trail and build a highway that would stretch from East to West  [CORRECT]
   b) Stop all westbound mail
   c) Abolish all railroads
   d) Sell the trail to private mines

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

43. True or False: Pioneers left behind guest books.

   a) True  [CORRECT]
   b) False

   Teach: Pioneers often carved their names and the date they traveled into surrounding rocks — what historians call giant stone guest books.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

44. True or False: Conestoga wagons were the wagon of choice for most pioneer families.

   a) True
   b) False  [CORRECT]

   Teach: Families typically chose the compact Prairie Schooner — it needed fewer oxen and survived rough terrain better. Conestogas were massive merchant wagons.

45. Donation land claims promised 360 acres of free land to single white males and this much land to married couples:

   a) 640 acres  [CORRECT]
   b) 120 acres
   c) 360 acres
   d) 1,200 acres

   Teach: Married couples could claim a full section — 640 acres — while single men received 360. That asymmetry shaped who got land.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

46. What was one of the most dangerous natural features pioneers faced on the trail?

   a) Quicksand pits inland
   b) Rivers — ferries cost money; fords could drown people, animals, and supplies  [CORRECT]
   c) Prairie fires only
   d) Sand dunes at sunset

   Teach: River crossings were the great choke point — pay a ferry, or ford and risk drowning people, stock, and cargo. Diaries cluster tragedy at water.

   b) About 1,000 miles

54. How long did it typically take to travel the Oregon Trail?

   a) About 4–6 months  [CORRECT]
   b) 1–2 months
   c) About a full year
   d) About two weeks

55. What was the nickname for the common canvas-topped wagons on the trail?

   a) Trail runners
   b) Desert ships
   c) Mountain movers

56. Which year is often called the peak 'flood' year of Oregon Trail travel?

   a) 1862
   b) 1872
   c) 1842
   d) 1852  [CORRECT]

   Teach: 1852 was the great flood year — more emigrants than any prior season, and the year the Meekers themselves crossed in.

57. Outfitting a family of four for the trail could easily run:

   a) $10 total
   b) $600–$1,200  [CORRECT]
   c) Nothing — the army paid everyone
   d) Millions in gold only

58. Practical guides warned against loading a wagon beyond roughly:

   a) About 2,000 pounds including people  [CORRECT]
   b) 10,000 pounds
   c) 500 pounds always
   d) No limit

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

59. Most companies tried to leave Missouri in:

   a) January
   b) April–May  [CORRECT]
   c) November
   d) August–September

   Teach: Spring departures let trains clear the mud and still beat the mountain snows.

60. A typical bulk good a family might bring on the Oregon Trail would be:

   a) Sushi
   b) Flour  [CORRECT]
   c) Jalapeños
   d) Garden greens

   a) Founding the Seattle Seahawks
   b) Commanding Fort Vancouver
   c) His work saving the Oregon Trail  [CORRECT]
   d) Inventing the first electric light

62. Many families, including the Meekers, crossed the Oregon Trail for the promise of:

   a) Free land  [CORRECT]
   b) Freedom from taxes

63. The Oregon Trail is actually made up of multiple starting points, branching trails, and ending points. When describing the trail, historians compare it to:

   a) A single paved highway
   b) A rope that is frayed on both ends  [CORRECT]
   c) One perfectly straight fence line
